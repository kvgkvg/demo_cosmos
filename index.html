How these captions were produced

The two tracks below come from two different mechanisms, and most of the findings trace back to that difference.

Qwen3.6 — one shot

The whole episode goes to a served Qwen3.6 in a single request, with the system prompt plus the episode's task instruction. One request, one caption track. Video frames dominate the input — 65,753 prompt tokens for a 7.2-second clip.

Cosmos3-Nano — two stages, chunked

Run locally through transformers, no server. A global context pass, then overlapping chunks stitched back into one track.

① Global pass

10 frames are cut with ffmpeg, one at the midpoint of each of 10 equal spans across the clip. They go in as images with the ask: one global caption, at most 3 sentences, no timestamps (≤256 tokens). This is context for every chunk that follows — it is how a chunk that only sees 20 seconds still knows what the episode is about.

② Chunk pass

The clip is cut into 20-second chunks with 1.5 seconds of overlap. If the leftover tail is shorter than 2× the overlap it is merged into the previous chunk instead of becoming a stub. Across this demo's 15 episodes that gives 1–4 chunks; the longest clip here (70.2s) splits like this:

0–20s
18.5–38.5s
37–57s
55.5–70.2s

Each chunk is re-encoded by ffmpeg and sampled at 2 fps. Every chunk request carries four things:

  1. the system prompt (system_prompt.txt) plus the episode's task instruction
  2. the global caption from stage ①
  3. the previous chunk's last caption line, for continuity
  4. an explicit instruction not to emit any line lying entirely inside the overlap — plus, on every chunk but the last, “if the final action is still in progress, omit that line, it will be captioned in the next chunk”

Stitch

The model answers in MM:SS.d – MM:SS.d: text lines, relative to the chunk. Any </think> reasoning is stripped, unparseable lines dropped, lines lying entirely in the overlap discarded, and the rest shifted back to global video time and clamped to the chunk's bounds. Lines that collapse to zero length are dropped, and an action the boundary cut in half is rejoined with its continuation, so the seams do not show. Sorted by start time, the result is written as one SRT — the same format as the ground truth.

Decoding is identical across chunks: bf16 · temperature 0.7 · top_p 0.8 · seed 0

Why it shows up below. Chunking is what bounds the damage: a bad chunk cannot poison the next one (no error accumulation), and no line can be emitted past the chunk's end (no overtime). It is also what causes the misses: chunks are captioned independently, so nothing enforces continuous coverage across a boundary (gap timestamps) or a consistent vocabulary between chunks (inconsistent context).
